## Goal

Stage 01 slice 5: the two things that make 1,500 phones safe to point at an audience, and the
harness that tells us whether the server survives them.

## What this slice is actually about

Everything so far assumed the server keeps working. This slice assumes it does not.

1. **Panic is the one operation that is not scheduled.** Every other change lands at a common future
   moment because synchrony matters. Panic lands now, because silence matters more than synchrony.
2. **A phone that stops hearing from the server must go quiet on its own.** If the control process
   dies mid-show, 1,500 phones holding a decoded buffer will happily keep playing to the end of the
   track with nobody able to stop them. The lease is the dead man's switch: the server keeps saying
   "you may make sound until time T", and a phone past its T mutes itself without being told to.
3. **Capacity claims need a measurement, not an argument.** 2,048 IDs allocated in a loop is not
   1,500 sockets, and neither is a number in a plan.

## Subplot: slice 5, in three reviewable steps

The contract already has `PanicRequest`, the `panic` server message and `lease.renew`
(`{ expiresServerMs }`). No schema change is proposed.

### Step 5A - panic

`POST /api/panic` is operator-only, takes a `commandId`, and is **immediate**: no lead time, no
preparation, no barrier. It cancels every pending action, forces the transport to stopped, and
broadcasts `panic` to every connected phone. It is idempotent by command ID like every other
command, and it is refused only for a wrong session, never for a stale revision: an operator
reaching for panic should never be told to reload first.

Tests: pending transport, assignment and mix actions are all cancelled; the transport is stopped
immediately rather than at a future time; every connected phone receives the broadcast; a stale
`expectedRevision` does not block it; a retry does not fire twice; it works while a cue is pending
and while playback is effective.

### Step 5B - the audio lease

The server renews a lease on a fixed interval, sending `lease.renew` with an `expiresServerMs` in
the near future. A phone compares that against its synchronized clock and mutes itself once it
passes, with no further message required. Panic expires the lease immediately as well as
broadcasting, so a phone that misses the panic broadcast still goes quiet on its own.

This is client-side enforcement by design: the server cannot reach a phone whose network has gone,
which is exactly the case the lease exists for. The server's job is only to keep saying "still fine"
while things are fine.

Tests: a renewal carries an expiry in the future and in the current epoch; the expiry advances on
each renewal; panic expires the lease immediately; a disconnected phone stops being renewed; the
renewal interval leaves room for at least one lost message before expiry.

### Step 5C - the load harness in `tools/load/`

A runnable harness that opens N simulated clients against a real backend and drives the concert
shape: a join wave, clock probes, readiness reports, a preparation and a scheduled cue, a
reassignment, and reconnects, all while an upload and an OTC job are running.

It reports: cue acknowledgements received before the deadline as a fraction of eligible clients,
event-loop delay percentiles sampled in the server process, join wave duration, reconnect success,
and every exclusion with its reason. It writes a text report under `.devcontext/evidence/`.

Tests: the harness runs at a small client count inside the gate so it cannot rot; the measurement
code is unit-tested against known inputs; the large run stays a manual command.

### Verification

`bun run gate:sync`, plus a real run at the largest client count this machine supports, reported
honestly with its limits.

### What this slice will not prove

A loopback socket is not a phone on venue Wi-Fi. This harness measures whether the **server**
survives 1,500 connections and still delivers cues on time. It says nothing about radio congestion,
NAT tables in the venue's access points, phones sleeping, or whether any sound comes out. The
masterplan is explicit that a 1,500-socket simulator is not 1,500 phones, and the report will say so
in those words.

## Open decisions for the lead

1. **How fast should phones go silent if the server dies?** The lease expiry is that answer. Five
   seconds means up to five seconds of unwanted audio after a failure; two seconds is tighter but a
   two-second network hiccup would mute the hall mid-piece. Proposal: renew every second, expire
   five seconds out, so four consecutive renewals must be lost before a phone goes quiet.
2. **What panic does to a phone.** Proposal: mute immediately **and** stop playback, rather than
   muting while the timeline keeps running underneath. Resuming then requires a deliberate new cue,
   which is the behaviour someone wants after hitting a red button.

## A constraint worth naming

`tools/load/` is not a workspace package, so it cannot resolve `@orchestra/sync`. Adding it to the
root workspaces changes the lockfile, and the root manifest belongs to the captain. Proposal: import
the estimator by relative path from `tools/load/` for now, which touches nothing shared, and propose

## Decisions taken by the lead

1. **Lease: renew every second, expire five seconds out.** Four consecutive renewals must be lost
   before a phone goes quiet, so an ordinary hiccup cannot mute a piece.
2. **Panic mutes and stops.** Coming back requires a deliberate new cue, which is what someone
   wants after hitting a red button.

### Step 5C: the load harness

`tools/load/` now holds a real harness: `client.ts` (a simulated phone using the **real** estimator),
`metrics.ts` (percentiles and honest rate formatting, unit-tested), and `index.ts` (join wave, cue,
reassignment, reconnects, upload and job contention, and a written report).

`backend/src/loop-lag.ts` samples timer drift inside the control process and reports it through
`/api/foundation`. Event-loop delay cannot be measured from outside: a client's round trip cannot
tell a busy server apart from a slow network.

## What the load run found

The harness was built to produce evidence and immediately produced four defects. Every one of them
was invisible to 180 passing tests.

1. **A snapshot re-validated every pending assignment on every read.** `pendingActions` rebuilt and
   re-parsed its contents each time it was accessed, and a snapshot is built on every `device.status`
   message. With 1,500 phones reporting every two seconds against a thousand pending assignments,
   this put event-loop p99 at **51.6 ms**, over the 50 ms target, with a 894 ms stall. Cached the
   assembled list and invalidated it on mutation: p99 fell to **1.8 ms**.
2. **Every join rewrote the entire checkpoint.** A join wave was quadratic: 1,500 joins each
   serialising a growing registry. `CheckpointStore.save` now coalesces callers that arrive while a
   write is merely queued, and closes the batch before the write begins so no caller is ever told
   its data is durable because someone else's write finished.
3. **A thousand-device assignment serialised a thousand payloads in one tick.** Now chunked with a
   yield between chunks, and payloads are built only for devices that actually hold a socket.
   Delivery is best effort because a missed broadcast is recoverable from a snapshot by design.
4. **The join rate limit was wrong, as suspected in slice 2.** At 300 burst / 200 per second, 1,500
   phones arriving together were shed 5,616 times and took 8.5 seconds of backoff to get in, while
   the server sat idle. Resized to a full session capacity of 2,048 with the same refill: the join
   wave now completes in **54 ms** with zero shedding. The number in the code is now measured rather
   than guessed, and the comment says which run produced it.

Two harness bugs were found the same way, and both were the harness modelling a client badly rather
than the server misbehaving: it gave up on a retryable 429 instead of backing off, and it did not
resnapshot after reconnecting, which made recovered clients look like missed cues.

A third lifecycle bug was found after the first passing evidence run: the timer read
`nextProbeDelayMs()` only when it was created, pinning every client to the 50 ms startup cadence for
five minutes instead of settling to 2500 ms after synchronization. That run over-stressed clock
traffic and passed, but it was not an accurate client model. The timer now schedules one probe at a
time and re-reads the estimator policy after every pair. The final report below was regenerated on
the corrected lifecycle.

### Worker-contention audit

The first five-minute report was invalid for one required dimension: `tools/load/index.ts` said it
ran under worker contention but only started an asset upload. The server itself had real job tests,
but the load scenario never called the job API. This was caught during the final audit before
handoff.

Added `tools/load/worker-fixture.ts`, an explicitly synthetic worker that consumes CPU in a separate
process for eight seconds, emits structured progress and writes a schema-valid result whose input
hashes match the server's manifest. The harness now creates/arms a 30-phone calibration, streams a
16 MB camera file, starts that worker, and records the worker's stage at the instant the transport
cue is sent. The fixture never reads a pixel and every result says `evidence: synthetic`; it is
contention evidence only.

A 30-client rehearsal against the real server confirmed the corrected scenario before the scale
run: the worker was at `validate` when the cue was sent and `complete` at the end; all 30 clients
acknowledged and knew the cue; three reconnects recovered; event-loop p99 was 1.56 ms.

### Final five-minute evidence

Authoritative report: [load-1500.md](../../evidence/sync-control/load-1500.md).

- 1,500/1,500 clients joined in a 61 ms wave; every socket was open by 337 ms and all 1,500 were
  still connected immediately before teardown.
- 1,500/1,500 acknowledged preparation before the deadline and were clock-ready when they did;
  1,500/1,500 knew the cue by its moment. Three recovered the cue from a snapshot after
  reconnecting rather than relying on the broadcast.
- The worker was at `validate` when the cue was sent and reached `complete`; the run also streamed
  the asset and camera uploads.
- Cue warning margin: p50 4985.2 ms, p95 4991.4 ms, p99 4991.7 ms.
- Event-loop delay measured inside the server: p50 1.02 ms, p95 1.62 ms, p99 **3.99 ms**, passing
  the `<50 ms` target. One isolated **209.17 ms maximum stall** remains. The target is p99, so the
  gate passes, but the maximum is retained as a limitation and should be correlated with process
  launch/assignment timing in a later profile rather than dismissed.
- 150 clients reconnected; 1,000/1,000 targeted assignments were committed to bass; transport was
  still playing at the end; no join was shed, no client error was recorded and no client was late
  or excluded. Fourteen sockets needed one or more opening retries during the initial connection
  wave and all recovered.

The report samples loopback sockets on this Mac. It is server-capacity evidence, not venue, phone,
browser or acoustic evidence.
